Що ми дізналися

  1. Mubit оцінює адаптер за схожістю з GPT, а не за правильністю. Людські виправлення в публічних docs у навчання не потрапляють, є лише кнопка Good/Bad. Без окремого механізму головний критерій суддів не закрити. Це питання №1 на kickoff.
  2. Справжній продукт хакатону — Middleware. Виправлення і правила лягають у «пам'ять застосунку». Детектор змін за нею запускає оновлення моделі. Їхній власний приклад: змінилась політика ескалації, і модель оновилась.
  3. Головна теза засновників — «It has been reminded, not improved». Нотатка в промпті модель не покращує, а оновлення ваг покращує. Демо будуємо саме на цьому контрасті.
  4. Конкуренти вмирають. OpenPipe закрився 30.07.2026, TensorZero теж згорнувся. OpenAI закриває fine-tuning, нові jobs можна запускати лише до 6.01.2027. Звідси сильний аргумент «own your model».
  5. Mubit — маленька компанія: близько 7 людей, близько $2.1M інвестицій, жодного публічного клієнта. Їм потрібні живі приклади використання, тому вертикальний продукт їм цінніший за ще одне демо дистиляції.

Як має виглядати демо

  1. Аналітик виправляє хіт: клієнт у статті не шахрай, а жертва.
  2. Reminded: наступний схожий хіт одразу правильний, бо виправлення лягло в пам'ять.
  3. Improved: адаптер v2 після виправлень проходить відкладений тестовий набір. Показуємо цифри: GPT, v1, v2.
  4. Fast refresh: MLRO змінює політику наживо, і gate не перемикає модель, поки нова версія не пройде тест.
  5. Ціна правильного рішення, затримка, частка трафіку на власній моделі.

How the product really works

  • Task = same system prompt + tools + tool choice + output format + model. Changing words in the prompt split the task; numbers, dates and IDs don't.
  • Session from metadata.session_id and similar. Test cases are held out by session.
  • First training at 25 kept examples. Not every call is kept.
  • Grading: "Matched {a}% of your provider model's answers · needs {need}%". Verdicts: Passed, Below target, Malformed tool calls, Prompt only, Not tested.
  • Limits: daily training limit (resets 01:00 London), a pause between trainings, no retry button, 3 adapter starts per org per day in Try models.
  • Serving: "There is no Mubit model ID". Yet Requests has a "Served by" column.
  • Feedback: only Good / Bad in the console. Import of past calls stops at the first live call.

Found outside the docs

  • mubit.ai/agents.md promises outcome attribution and prompt optimization and links to four docs pages that now 404 F.
  • The memory SDK still ships: mubit-sdk 0.13.2 with remember, recall, record_outcome, feedback, optimize_prompt F.
  • Middleware: memory is "the source of record"; a generator makes a small update; a change detector triggers a refresh, e.g. when an escalation policy changes F. Related research: Text-to-LoRA, Internalizer.

Implication I. If our reviewer corrects GPT, the adapter is still graded on matching GPT. Without an event-specific path, corrections do nothing or lower the pass rate.

A path that works regardless: store each correction as a precedent and put it in the user message of the next similar call. GPT then answers the corrected way ("reminded"), and Mubit distils those answers into the next adapter ("improved").

Mubit vs the field

Strong (documented)

Automatic task discovery by fingerprint. Honest gate with a "Prompt only" verdict. First training at 25 examples. Memory and adapters in one loop.

Weak or unproven

No measured results in public. Pass = imitation, while the site promises "does better". Serving, export and privacy undocumented. Tiny team. Crowded "stop renting intelligence" slogan.

Gaps nobody fills well

  1. Proof before switching, graded on outcomes, with rollback on drift.
  2. Deciding which layer to fix: memory, prompt, route or weights.
  3. Cost per successful task, not cost per token.
  4. Self-serve post-training for teams with no ML staff.
  5. Refugees from OpenAI fine-tuning before 6 Jan 2027.
  6. Portability: exportable adapters, data and eval history.

A. Threshold, the recommendation

The task. Screening matches a customer to a news article. An analyst decides: same person? Subject, victim or witness? Allegation, charge or conviction? In scope? Recent enough? Then discount or escalate, with a rationale for the audit file.

£38.3bnUK financial crime compliance spend, 2023; 66% on due diligence F
85–95%screening false positives (vendor-reported) F
69%of AI false positives were over-flagging marginal evidence (KYBench) F
1.5%were entity confusion. Hallucination: 0% F
  • Why corrections matter: the frontier error is calibration to a firm's risk appetite. Taktile's advice is to build the queue around "threshold adjudication". Analyst corrections teach exactly that I.
  • Written vs tacit policy: the system prompt holds the vague written policy. Ground truth follows the analysts' tacit rules (victims discounted, civil disputes out, events over 6 years discounted unless sanctions). GPT over-flags; corrections fix it.
  • Data: synthetic customers and articles about fictional people, generated through Mubit as a separate task. Labels come from a deterministic policy in code. No real people in Mubit's recorder.
  • Edge cases: namesakes with wrong age or city, customer as victim, acquittals, civil disputes, traffic offences, 10-year-old events, company vs director, transliterated names, several people in one article.
  • Track 3: MLRO changes risk appetite live ("allegation-stage PEP articles now escalate"). Their own Middleware example is an escalation-policy change.
  • Track 2: frontier vs adapter on held-out cases, with escalation recall first, since a missed true hit is the one error that cannot be tolerated.
  • Risks: synthetic data too clean; a human must stay accountable; incumbents (LexisNexis, Moody's, ComplyAdvantage, Silent Eight, Quantexa, Taktile).

B. Complaint triage, the runner-up

  • Task: DISP complaint or not, FCA product and cause, vulnerability drivers, 3-day vs 8-week clock, ombudsman-uphold risk.
  • Volume: 1.74m complaints at FCA firms in H2 2025; 305,726 new ombudsman complaints in 2024/25 F.
  • Data: CFPB/consumer-finance-complaints on Hugging Face, CC0, real narratives with product and issue labels (US, noisy).
  • Do not scrape ombudsman decisions: its terms forbid storing site material in any retrieval system without permission F.

F. Why tenancy is weak

  1. The correction loop conflicts with imitation grading.
  2. Not really recurring: the spike was the 31 May 2026 information-sheet deadline.
  3. The correction moment is staged: with the rules in the prompt, GPT likely catches the planted fee.
  4. Already served: Landlord HQ £9.99/mo, Contract Icks £49/scan, RightTo.Rent £99/property, LeaseScan, HomeLet, PAM, HomeThink.
  5. Weak privacy case; judges already rewarded a renting agent ("Rentry", May 2026).

Keep from the brief: frozen prompt and schema, one session per document, synthetic labels from code, a record-off eval set, v1 before 01:00, warm adapter, backup video.

Threshold. Every "discount or escalate" decision your analysts make becomes your own model. It clears adverse-media hits the way your team would, learns from each correction, and keeps customer data off third-party APIs.

Pipeline, all calls through Mubit

Task 1 · heroHit disposition: profile + article → same person, role, stage, category, year, escalate / discount / needs_review, reason code
Task 2File note: audit rationale. Likely passes fast or returns "Prompt only", which shows the gate
Task 3 · dataArticle generator, separate prompt, only to build the dataset

Two-speed learning loop

Reminded · seconds

Analyst corrects. The correction is stored as a precedent (Mubit memory or our DB). The next similar hit gets it in the user message, so the frontier answer is already right.

Improved · next refresh

Mubit trains the next adapter on the corrected traffic. On held-out cases, without precedents, v2 handles the pattern. This is the weight update the judges asked for.

If Mubit confirms a direct corrections API at kickoff, use it for the slow lane and keep precedents as the fast lane.

Business model

Mubit's

Usage through the gateway plus adapter training and hosting; it keeps part of the gap between frontier and open-model cost. Risks: imitation ceiling, frontier price cuts, undocumented serving and export, a 7-person team against funded rivals. Openings: OpenAI refugees, regulated sectors, "which layer to fix".

Threshold's

Buyer: MLRO or head of fin-crime ops at UK fintechs, EMIs and challenger banks. Price per hit or per screened customer, plus hosting of the client's own model. ROI = hits × minutes saved × analyst cost − fees. Moat: each client's adapters, calibration history and audit trail, never pooled. Next: sanctions hits, transaction alerts, complaints.

What Mubit rewards. A learning loop you can see on stage, for a real person or small business, with a small stack and numbers. That has not changed since April. What is new tonight: the loop must be a model update, not memory.
